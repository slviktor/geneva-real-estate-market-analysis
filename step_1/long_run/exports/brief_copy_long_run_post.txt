Geneva real estate: what 35 years of official data tell a potential buyer

I analyzed 35 years of Geneva property transactions — not listings or appraisals, but completed notarial sales (OCSTAT). Then cross-validated with Wüest Partner hedonic indices and BIS/BFS national benchmarks.

Here's what the data actually shows.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

THE LONG GAME

▸ Geneva canton apartments: +2.5%/yr nominal, +1.4%/yr above inflation since 1990
▸ Geneva city: +2.9%/yr — prices roughly double every 25 years
▸ Houses: +2.4%/yr canton, +2.8%/yr Lake Geneva region
▸ Three independent sources (OCSTAT, Wüest, BIS) — correlation 0.99 on levels. The trend is real.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

THE RISK NO ONE TALKS ABOUT

▸ Geneva had two full correction cycles
▸ Worst case: –25% from peak, 14 years to break even (city, 1990–2004)
▸ Second cycle: –12%, 9 years to recover (canton, 2012–2021)
▸ Switzerland nationally dropped only –10% — Geneva amplifies the cycle 2×

But: every new peak exceeded the previous one.
If you can hold through a downturn, history rewarded patience.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

WHAT'S PUSHING PRICES NOW

▸ Construction costs in Geneva: +18% since 2020 (faster than CPI)
▸ New builds get more expensive to deliver → supports existing property values
▸ Typical existing apartment that sells: ~120 m² — unchanged for 18 years
▸ New builds: ~140 m² and getting larger — developers target the premium end

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

PRACTICAL TAKEAWAY

If you're considering buying in Geneva:

→ Budget for a full cycle. Breaking even may take 8–14 years if you buy at the wrong time.
→ Think real returns, not nominal: +1.4–1.9%/yr after inflation — solid, not spectacular.
→ "New is cheaper" at canton level is an illusion — composition effect. Within each commune, new costs 17–29% more.
→ Rising construction costs = structural tailwind for existing property owners.
